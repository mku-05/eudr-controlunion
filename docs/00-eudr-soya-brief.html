1 · The brief as received
"Control Union works with soya farming and needs to pass regulations like EUDR. They may provide a before and after image of land and we need to derive that no deforestation happened."

Informal, second-hand. Everything below is interpretation; §10 lists what must be confirmed.

2 · What is actually being asked for

Control Union is a certification and inspection body, not a farmer or trader. Its clients — soya producers, crushers, exporters and EU importers — must prove to the EU that their soya is EUDR-compliant, and CU wants to be the party that verifies it. So the product is almost certainly:

An audit-grade platform that takes the geolocation of soya plots, independently verifies with satellite data that no forest on those plots was converted to agriculture after 31 Dec 2020, and produces evidence Control Union can put its accreditation behind.

The "before and after image" is the client's picture of the output — the land in 2020 versus now. It is not the input. The input is a plot polygon. The system generates the dated imagery as evidence.

Client-supplied photos cannot be primary evidence: their date, location and authenticity are unverifiable, and neither a competent authority nor CU's accreditation body would accept them. They can be attached as supplementary material (drone shots with EXIF GPS, field-visit photos), nothing more.

4 · Re-framing "before / after" into the real technical problem

For each plot polygon, answer three questions with evidence:

  1. Was there forest (per EUDR definition) inside this polygon on 31 Dec 2020? If not, the plot is compliant on deforestation grounds — it was already cropland or pasture. Most Brazilian soya plots land here. This is the cheap, high-volume path.
  2. If yes, was any of it lost between 1 Jan 2021 and the production date? Published loss and alert datasets, plus the platform's own change detection on the Sentinel time series.
  3. If lost, was it converted to agricultural use? Post-loss land-cover classification and visual check. Conversion means non-compliant.
GREEN

No EUDR forest in 2020, or forest intact through the production date. Automated; spot-checked.

AMBER

Loss signal inside the polygon but cause or land use ambiguous. Goes to an analyst. Never auto-resolved.

RED

Forest at baseline, loss after cut-off, converted to crop or pasture. Analyst-confirmed only.

Each verdict carries: area affected (ha), confidence, datasets and versions consulted, dated image chips, time-series chart, analyst notes, sign-off.

Why one before/after pair is not enough

  • Cloud. Mato Grosso and the Cerrado have months of cloud; a clean optical pair near the required dates may not exist. Sentinel-1 radar and multi-year time series solve this.
  • Seasonality. Soya harvest, pasture burn and dry season all read as "vegetation loss" in NDVI. A time series separates a crop cycle from clearing.
  • Provenance. The auditor must state sensor, date and processing. Only platform-generated imagery carries that.
  • Definition. An image shows tree loss; the law asks about forest per Art. 2(4) converted to agriculture. That needs classified layers, not eyeballing.

7 · Functional scope

Core — the product does not exist without these

  1. Plot and supplier ingest. GeoJSON, Shapefile, KML, CSV, CAR. Validation: topology, self-intersections, area vs declared, duplicates, cross-supplier overlaps, inside declared municipality, ≥ 4 ha ⇒ polygon required. Versioned.
  2. Deforestation screening engine. Automated GREEN / AMBER / RED per plot, per §4; batch; re-run on new alerts; area and confidence per plot.
  3. Evidence generation. Dated before (≤ 2020-12-31) and after (near production date) chips with sensor, cloud and date metadata; NDVI + radar time-series chart; loss overlay; PDF/JSON pack; content hash and timestamp.
  4. Analyst review workflow. Queue, assignment, override with justification, second reviewer, sign-off, immutable log.
  5. Status roll-up and export. Plot → supplier → lot/shipment → client; DDS-ready GeoJSON in the EU IS format, production dates, quantities.

Second wave

  1. Art. 10 risk assessment. Country and sub-national risk, forest presence, indigenous peoples, corruption, supply-chain complexity, local deforestation prevalence, credible concerns, sanctions; mitigation actions; record.
  2. Legality dossier. Per-supplier checklist and documents; automated overlays with embargo, indigenous and protected-area layers.
  3. Volume reconciliation. Declared tonnes vs plot area × regional yield. Flags over-declaration — the standard way non-compliant soya is laundered through compliant plots. Most satellite tools ignore it; a certifier cannot.
  4. Chain of custody. Segregation vs mass balance at silo, crusher and port; lots linked to plots.
  5. DDS submission. TRACES API; reference-number management; downstream referencing.
  6. Continuous monitoring. Re-screen the plot base on new RADD / GLAD / DETER alerts; notify.

Non-functional that matter here

  • Audit-grade: every verdict reproducible — dataset version, algorithm version, analyst, timestamp.
  • Multi-tenant with strict isolation; CU's clients are competitors, and farmers' polygons are commercially sensitive.
  • Scale: one trader can hold thousands of plots of hundreds to thousands of hectares. Screening is batch and cheap; imagery is pulled only for AMBER/RED and for evidence.
  • EN / PT-BR / ES. Five-year retention. LGPD and GDPR.
  • Commodity-agnostic core — cocoa, coffee, palm, rubber and wood share the engine; only legality layers and forest-type nuances change.
8 · Hard problems to be honest about
Cerrado

Most new Brazilian soya expansion is in the Cerrado (MATOPIBA). Much of it is not "forest" under EUDR, so the product may correctly say GREEN while NGOs and buyers say the soya comes from converted native vegetation. Recommend two flags — EUDR forest and native vegetation — so the legal answer and the reputational answer are both visible.

Baseline disagreement

JRC GFC 2020, Hansen and WorldCover disagree at plot edges. Document a precedence rule (JRC primary; Hansen and a 2020 Sentinel-2 composite as tie-breakers) and route conflicts to review.

Plot precision

A CAR polygon covers the whole property including the legal reserve. One hectare of reserve cleared in 2022 turns a 5,000 ha GREEN farm RED. Suppliers must be able to submit field-level polygons, and the client must see exactly what the overlap is.

False positives

Harvest, fire, drought, cloud shadow, eucalyptus and tree-crop rotations. Time series + radar + an analyst gate. RED is never fully automatic.

Polygon acquisition

Farmers resist handing over polygons; traders often know the silo, not the farm. Make CAR-based onboarding trivial: search by CAR number → fetch polygon → confirm.

Timeline

110 days to application. Ship screening + evidence + review first; risk, legality and DDS after. Build on Earth Engine or Copernicus; do not train models.

Liability

CU puts its accreditation behind each verdict. A versioned methodology document is a deliverable, not just software.

9 · Suggested MVP — to validate with CU
Upload polygons → screen against JRC GFC 2020 + Hansen + RADD/GLAD + PRODES → GREEN / AMBER / RED per plot with dated Sentinel-2 chips and NDVI/S1 series → analyst review and sign-off → evidence PDF + DDS-ready GeoJSON/CSV

Single tenant: one CU office, one pilot client, Brazil first.

10 · Questions to put to Control Union
  1. Who is the primary user — CU auditors verifying clients, or CU's clients self-serving on a CU-branded platform? Both?
  2. What does CU deliver to its client at the end — a verification statement, an audit report, an input into RTRS / ISCC / ProTerra / Cefetra CRS audits?
  3. Which origins first — Brazil (which states and biomes), Argentina, Paraguay, Uruguay, India, US?
  4. Volume in season one: clients, suppliers, plots, hectares. Peak timing.
  5. Where do polygons come from today — CAR, client GIS, nothing? Is a field-capture mobile app needed?
  6. Scope: deforestation only, or deforestation + legality + Art. 10 risk assessment?
  7. DDS submission to TRACES, or only the evidence for the client's own filing?
  8. Should the platform also flag non-EUDR conversion — Cerrado native vegetation, Soy Moratorium 2008 cut-off?
  9. Budget for commercial imagery, or free Sentinel/Landsat only?
  10. What happens on RED — supplier exclusion, CAPA, re-audit? Who decides?
  11. Existing tools or partners (Global Forest Watch Pro, Satelligence, LiveEO, Orbify, Meridia)? Build, buy or integrate?
  12. Other commodities on the roadmap?
  13. Data ownership, confidentiality, hosting region (EU / Brazil), LGPD and GDPR posture.
