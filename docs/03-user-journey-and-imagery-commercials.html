Part 1

The user journey, in plain language

An exporter sends Control Union a messy pile of files. The auditor drops that pile into the console, the engine does a day's worth of checking in minutes, and the auditor spends their time only on the handful of things that genuinely need a human eye.
1

A package arrives

Exporter

An exporter wants to ship 15,500 t of soya to Rotterdam. They send what they have: a supplier spreadsheet, a lot spreadsheet, a GeoJSON/KML of their fields, a purchase contract, a chain-of-custody manifest, a traceability certificate, and a blurry scanned CAR receipt. Some of it is in Portuguese. None of it is tidy.

2

The auditor drops it in

Auditor

On the Cases page they drag the whole folder onto the upload box and click Create case. That is the only data entry they ever do.

3

The engine reads everything

EngineIntake agent
  • PDFs are read; scanned pages go through OCR. Every important value — CAR number, tax ID, tonnes, dates, "no deforestation after 2020" declarations — is located on the page and boxed so it can be cited later.
  • The Intake agent works out who the suppliers are, which fields belong to whom, what is in each lot, and lists what is missing (e.g. "No-Data Cooperative sent no polygons").
  • Every polygon is checked: valid geometry, inside Brazil, polygon where one is required, no overlap with another supplier's field.
4

Every field is checked against satellites

Engine

For each polygon the engine asks three questions: Was there forest here on 31 Dec 2020? Has any of it been lost since? Was that land turned into farmland? It uses public datasets — Hansen forest change, ESA WorldCover, Sentinel-2 — and produces a flag:

PASS · no forest at cut-off, or intactEXCEPTION · changed but ambiguousCRITICAL · forest cleared after cut-off, now crop or pasture

Alongside the flag it saves the evidence: a before and after satellite image, a colour map of what changed, and a vegetation (NDVI) timeline.

5

An AI analyst looks at the hard ones

Geo Analyst agent

For every EXCEPTION and CRITICAL plot, a vision model reads those images and writes an opinion — "this looks like a harvest cycle, not clearing" or "cleared block visible in the after-image, cropped since". It recommends; it never decides.

6

The rest of the package is checked too

EngineLegality · Drafter · Critic agents
  • Legality — fields overlaid on indigenous lands, protected areas and embargo lists; documents read for missing paperwork.
  • Chain of custody — the manifest becomes a flow, farm → silo → exporter, and tonnes are balanced at every step. A silo that received 1,300 t but shipped 1,500 t is flagged.
  • Volume — declared tonnes against what those hectares could plausibly yield.
  • Risk — an Art. 10 score with a drafted narrative.
  • Critic — re-reads the whole case looking for anything that would embarrass the signer.
7

The auditor opens the case

Auditor

The case page shows a satellite map of the fields coloured by flag, a risk gauge and a short "What needs attention" list. Most of the package is already green and needs nothing.

8

The auditor works the review queue

Auditor

In the Review workspace they step through the three or four flagged fields. For each one they see the before/after slider, the change map, the NDVI chart and the AI's opinion, and make one of two calls: Approve the engine's flag, or Override it with a written reason. Both are recorded against their name on a tamper-evident ledger.

9

They check the exceptions

Auditor

The Exception ledger lists everything else — the silo gap, the embargoed supplier, the missing polygons, the over-declared lot — each linked to the exact line in the PDF it came from. The Documents view shows those citations boxed on the actual page.

10

They acknowledge

Auditor

Once every flagged field has a decision, the auditor clicks Acknowledge dossier. The engine refuses if anything is still unreviewed or the Critic found a blocking issue that has not been accepted. The output is an evidence-pack PDF and an exception CSV for the exporter and Control Union's file.

11

The fields stay watched

Engine

If a deforestation alert later fires inside one of those polygons, the engine re-screens it and reopens the case.

Who does what

The engine

Reading, geometry, satellite maths, mass balances. Deterministic and reproducible — the part that carries the evidence.

The AI agents

Judgement-shaped work: extracting from messy files, looking at imagery, drafting, criticising. They only ever recommend.

The auditor

Every decision that matters, with their name on it. Approve, override, acknowledge.

Per the pilot SOW: nothing here is a certificate or a conformity decision. It is evidence preparation that lets an accredited human decide faster and with better proof.

Part 2

Commercial imagery: how it is bought and how it is packaged

Commercial imagery is a by-exception, pass-through cost — not a platform cost. Free Sentinel-2 at 10 m settles most plots; sharp imagery is bought only for the flagged plots an auditor wants to defend, and is priced per plot.

How to package it

Included
$0 every plot, unlimited

Sentinel-2 + Hansen + WorldCover screening, evidence chips, NDVI series. This is the product.

High-resolution verification
$95–150 per plot per date

Ordered from the plot drawer with one click, price shown before ordering. SkySat archive at cost ($25–60); margin covers ordering, alignment, storage and licence. Attached to the evidence pack with licence metadata on the ledger.

Continuous monitoring
~$50–60 per km² per year, entry tier

PlanetScope area-under-management over Control Union's active supplier base: daily 3 m coverage of every plot for alerts. Sensible once more than ~500 plots are under watch.

Tasking
per order client approval

Never bundled into a flat fee — unpredictable and cloud-dependent. Charged per order, for disputes.

What it means for the pilot

Nothing in the $0 pilot SOW changes. Commercial imagery belongs in the Year 1 enterprise conversion as a metered line ("HR verification, per plot-date") plus an optional monitoring subscription. Reserve a small imagery budget (~$500) inside the pilot to show one flagged plot at 50 cm — the demo moment where the auditor sees the cleared block, not a 10 m blur.

Integration is already seamed: a provider in the engine fetches a scene by bounds and date; SkySat and Vantor both have ordering APIs, so "Order HR imagery" becomes a button that creates the order, waits for delivery and attaches the chips as evidence.
